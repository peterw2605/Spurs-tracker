How the probability is worked out

Each rumour is scored from the headlines that mention it. Four things move the number:

  • Source reliability. Every outlet sits in a tier, from tier 1 (the outlets whose transfer stories are rarely wrong) down to tier 4 (aggregators and unranked blogs).
  • Language strength. “medical booked” counts for far more than “monitoring”, and “not for sale” counts against.
  • Corroboration. Independent outlets agreeing raises the number, with diminishing returns — twenty tabloids repeating each other is not twenty confirmations.
  • Recency. Evidence decays with a nine-day half-life, so a three-week-old link fades on its own.

Only the strongest headline from each outlet counts, and headlines from multi-club live blogs are treated as a mention only, because a phrase like “done deal” in them usually belongs to another club’s transfer.

These numbers come from press coverage, not from inside the club. Treat them as a read on what the media is reporting, not a forecast — and check the linked articles before believing any of it.
